# 22–22a London Street

The narrow Art Deco building immediately south of the former Britannia/Co-operative Bank at 20 London Street. Current address/use is verified as Merkur Slots; the model deliberately follows the visible **May 2011 Money Shop frontage**, as a declared historical storefront. It does not claim to reproduce the present tenant's fitout or the glazing alterations associated with the 2019 residential conversion.

## Evidence and registration

The retained Google Street View photograph shows the tall continuous glazed shaft, pale stone piers with narrow vertical relief, fine horizontal steel framing, rounded projecting terminal and yellow shop fascia. The shared aerial shows a long narrow flat roof and square rooflights. Source links, dates and limitations are in `research/references.json`; no reference pixels are included in Git.

The Fitton Estates brochure text, updated 11 January 2023, identifies a four-storey stone/glazed building with six upper flats and an independent entrance at 22a. Its ground-floor area of approximately 137 square metres is consistent with a narrow, deep parcel. Only cached brochure text was available: its photographs and plans were **not** inspected. The FSA address record confirms the current Merkur identity.

Coordinates retain the common memorial origin and 43.5293-degree site yaw. Local frontage X is −11.8772; Y extends from −95.85 at the shared edge with 20 to an estimated −101.25. The block has the same 0.02418-radian registration adjustment about (−11.8772, −68.7886), and ground datum 0.7845 m. Frontage width 5.4 m, depth 27 m, height 13.1 m and rooflight dimensions are visually calibrated estimates, not survey measurements. The building at 20 owns its southern hip; 22 has its own flat roof and taller party wall.

## Construction and viewing levels

The upper glass has a real continuous opening behind seven curved chordal strips per storey, with dark spandrel bands, swept stone returns and eighteen small inset lights. Detail and Walk retain stone reveals, rails, stepped piers and the recessed entrance. District preserves their main silhouette with baked surface detail. Roof deck, parapets and low rooflights are closed meshes. Ground/paving belongs to the public-realm asset.

Detail and Walk retain their authored geometry and existing sign bake. District is an independent envelope described below; no whole-building decimation is used.

## Validation and remaining uncertainty

The full asset build checks packed procedural maps, finite UV meshes, unobscured glazed openings, closed cap/deck/rooflights, and registration/shared boundary with 20. Isolated publication classifies every mesh and validates all twelve part/level GLBs. Exported Detail/Walk/District front, oblique and roof renders are reviewed; combined viewer publication remains the coordinator's final check.

The exact independent 22a door location is unresolved in the retained historical view and is deferred rather than cut into a guessed pier. Rear windows, rear wall materials and precise roof levels require better ground or aerial evidence; no speculative rear window grid is added. Current shop signage and post-2019 glazing are also deferred. Hidden walls are conservative envelopes, not a claim of complete architectural survey. No interiors are modelled.

## Independent audit, 22 October 2026

A clearer frontal photograph from [Modern Mooch](https://modernmooch.com/2025/04/03/southport-walk/) supersedes the apparent asymmetry in the oblique 2011 view. The final geometry has matching lower stone shoulders and a cylindrical central glass bay. Seven chordal strips per storey retain the rounded silhouette, with dark intermediate bands, swept returns containing eighteen actual small window apertures, slim rounded ribs and semicircular capitals. The projecting crown follows the round bay. The historical pale left entrance remains based on 2011 imagery; its use as shop or upper-floor access is unresolved. This asset deliberately combines the more legible permanent architectural evidence with a declared historical shopfront, rather than claiming a current Merkur fitout.

The hidden roof remains below the observed shoulders; its exact height is estimated. Side-wall end caps terminate within the rear wall instead of overlapping its outer surface. Validation checks all21 curved glazing panels,18 return lights, visible left entrance, centred crown and non-overlapping rear-wall junction, alongside the original closure and registration checks.

The earlier architectural audit checked the full build, all twelve GLBs and front, oblique and roof renders. Detail and Walk retain the glazing assemblies; District retains the curvature with seven continuous baked receiver strips. Fascia lettering is baked at reduced levels. No reference image pixels are published.

Local viewer reports caught the curved bay's base overlapping the historical
fascia and an open entrance cheek. The base now clears the fascia crown, with a
closed underside and a slightly shorter lowest glazed tier; upper floor and
crown levels are unchanged. Side returns and a soffit close the existing pale
recessed doorway. The retained frontal photograph establishes the separation
above the fascia; exact clearances remain estimated. Validation checks vertical
clearance and rays across/up the door recess, and all LoDs are checked from both
reported camera positions.

A later oblique snag exposed the stepped overlap between the fascia board and
its backing, plus a 15 mm slit above the door rail. The board now has one full
rectangular outline with its backing meeting the rear face; the transom meets
the rail. Depth and entrance visibility probes cover these joins.

## Current District envelope

The cylindrical glazed shaft keeps its seven-chord silhouette, swept returns and projecting crown. The long flat roof, parapets and recessed entrance remain geometric. Fine joinery, ribs, cap moulding, lettering and rooflights are represented by the projected maps; rooflights no longer protrude at District distance. This uses curved receivers rather than flattening the distinctive bay into a facade plane.

The four parts use architecture 256 × 128, roof 128 × 128, shopfront 256 × 64 and rear 128 × 128 atlases, each with colour, tangent normal and roughness maps. Topology and atlas dimensions are independent of added Detail geometry. The common geographic transform and existing dimensional estimates are unchanged.

Published total: **168 triangles, 90,540 bytes including textures, 1,330,750 estimated GPU bytes**. Source construction/validation, float32 receiver overlap checks, sampled projection, actual bake coverage and normal validity, and export budgets pass. Visual acceptance is delegated to the project owner for this LoD rollout; no new architectural evidence or visual inspection is claimed.
