# Embassy Restaurant, 24–26 Nevill Street

The Embassy occupies the chamfered Nevill/Stanley corner of the combined Silcock block. The model represents the May–July 2026 exterior, with the operator and Sefton licensing record establishing its identity. Reference photographs remain private; links and coverage are in the asset's research directory.

Four loaded Google 3D orbits and a scaled overhead view establish a hipped main roof, a rear gable, and stepped flat service roofs. May 2026 Maps place photography supplies the principal facade; July 2026 car panoramas supply the corner entrance and Stanley elevations. A further rear panorama initially rendered black, then loaded and revealed the lower service range beside the garden wall. Mapillary loaded controls but no map imagery; indexed searches yielded no useful additional target image.

Dimensions are estimates, not a survey: 11.6 m overall frontage, 17.6 m main roof depth, approximately 35.9 m total side depth, 10.5 m eaves and 13.6 m ridge above the local building base. The measured chamfer is about 2 m wide, substantially smaller than the combined OSM seed's corner cut. The main frontage has two wide window bays, narrow corner windows, diamond brick panels, cream sash frames, burgundy fascia/awnings and a recessed corner door. The Stanley side changes to plain brick, blocked lower openings and service pipework. Private rear courts have limited coverage; hidden ornament and windows are not invented.

The old `east-amusement-block` placeholder is partially cut back, preserving the Flavours/amusement remainder. A 1.48 m² residual strip at the far service end, only approximately 0.1–0.2 m wide, is retired as a difference between the seed and the newly registered wall positions. It is not treated as a separate building. This discrepancy is smaller than the recorded plan uncertainty.

## Viewing levels

Detail retains individual soldier bricks, diamond relief, sash bars, pipework and lettering. Walk omits fine glazing bars, soldier-brick subdivisions and small drainpipes while retaining main frames, signs and awnings. District uses independent wall/roof receivers and awning silhouettes, with fixed 512×512, 512×256 and 256×256 atlases. Its 8.5 MB GPU budget includes the approximately 7.34 MB three-map atlas floor; download and triangle budgets are enforced separately.

## Review and limitations

Initial front/corner/roof renders were compared with the reference views before ornament. The comparison corrected the oversized seed chamfer and retained the main roof direction and stepped service masses. A subsequent render caught reversed glyph orientation in the reflected site frame; corrected lettering now reads from the street and sits outside its sign backing. The full master validator checks all shell/trim triangles for finite coordinates, degeneracy and same-facing coplanar overlaps, including repeated joinery and lettering.

Final exported Detail, Walk and District have been inspected from the corner, with the Detail rear/roof checked separately. The final exported audit finds no cross-asset overlap involving Embassy and none in District. Two Detail/Walk numerical residues remain: an approximately 9-micrometre-wide party/service wall seam (0.0000855 m²), and a sill contact of 0.00000319 m². These are recorded numerical boundary residues, not a blanket exemption for visible conflicts. The assembled original report viewpoint settled with the actual Walk tile loaded and zero pending/failed requests. At the front pavement the actual Detail tile loaded; switching to Walk retained position and grounded the camera at 1.76497 m eye height. Frontage, awning clearance, ground contact and placeholder join were inspected in that scene. Final renders also passed. Outdoor tables, chairs, menu photographs, interiors and inaccessible rear details are not reconstructed in this pass. The small tenant script is a typeface approximation, not a facsimile logo. Weathering is procedural, not photographed material.
