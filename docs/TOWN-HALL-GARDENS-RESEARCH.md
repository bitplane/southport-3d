# Town Hall / Princess Diana Gardens: research in progress

The marked area in snag `bb62fe7cdf6648698c60acfd010d6093` extends from the
Christ Church/Town Hall approach to the Eastbank Street junction, including the
garden, paths, boundaries and adjoining street surfaces. The corridor public
realm owns its ground; the two existing polygonal pavilion placeholders are
`way-371196091` and `way-1370054624`. This research describes the earlier
arrangement, not a survey or a prediction of the regeneration scheme.

## Evidence reviewed 26 October 2026

- [Google Maps satellite](https://www.google.com/maps/@53.64743,-3.0064,19z)
  and [3D view](https://www.google.com/maps/@53.645987,-3.0064,195a,35y,39.49t/data=!3m1!1e3): overhead and oblique photogrammetry show a broad paved civic
  space, a circular centre and axial patterned paving, pavilion roofs at either
  end, mature trees and a service/parking route beside the Atkinson. Capture
  date is unknown and canopies conceal portions of the ground.
- [Graham Robson's photograph, 24 March 2018](https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/5720570), looking northeast, shows large flags, a checker-patterned axial
  strip, the circular stepped centre, benches, globe lamps, balustrades and the
  north pavilion. The exact dimensions and counts cannot be measured from it.
- [Stephen Craven's photograph, 22 September 2025](https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/8160819), looking north, shows the shallow fountain pool, concentric
  paved surfaces and temporary timber planters. Do not assume these planter
  positions apply to the 2018 scene.
- [Google Street View, July 2019](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&viewpoint=53.64760,-3.00670&heading=110&pitch=0)
  shows the Lord Street side, mature trees, rows of dark benches and a pale
  entrance with columns and balustrades. [Eastbank Street approach](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&viewpoint=53.64718,-3.00715&heading=100&pitch=0)
  shows the south pavilion as a glazed polygonal building with a shallow
  multi-faceted roof and dark structural framing; furniture and bus shelter
  remain separate from the garden.
- [Sefton Council's conservation appraisal](https://modgov.sefton.gov.uk/moderngov/Data/Planning%20Committee/20030312/Agenda/Appendix%2015.pdf)
  describes sandstone flags with ornamental Portland and Westmoreland stone,
  low walls, urns, balustrades, globe lamps and entrance columns. The [council's
  regeneration page](https://www.sefton.gov.uk/southport-together/town-hall-gardens-regeneration)
  describes subsequent temporary improvements and proposals for change.

## Working measurements and uncertainty

The pre-existing attributed OSM-derived site outline places the landscaped
reservation roughly between site X -243 and -116 m and site Y -18 and 18 m;
the circular route is centred near (-186.7, -1) m. These values are alignment
inputs, not surveyed measurements. The working paved correction spans X -245 to
-136 m and Y -25 to 25 m, retaining the existing bounded elevation controls.
The pavilion footprints are seed outlines with 1–3 m estimated uncertainty.
Precise levels, stair/ramp access, furniture placements, boundary openings,
planting and individual pavilion elevations need closer evidence and checking
against neighbouring exported meshes. No inscription has been read.

## Status

The first ground correction replaces the broad provisional lawn inside the
bounded civic area with paving. It does **not** yet reconstruct the fountain,
checkerwork, pavilions, stonework, planting, or street furniture. It must not be
presented as completing the snag. Ground, accessibility, the two placeholder
buildings and all three assembled viewer levels still require detailed work and
visual review.
