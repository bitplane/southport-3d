# Town Hall / Princess Diana Gardens: research in progress

The marked area in snag `bb62fe7cdf6648698c60acfd010d6093` extends from the
Christ Church/Town Hall approach to the Eastbank Street junction, including the
garden, paths, boundaries and adjoining street surfaces. The corridor public
realm owns its ground; the two existing polygonal pavilion placeholders are
`way-371196091` and `way-1370054624`. This research describes the earlier
arrangement, not a survey or a prediction of the regeneration scheme.

## Working measurements and uncertainty

The pre-existing attributed OSM-derived site outline places the landscaped
reservation roughly between site X -243 and -116 m and site Y -18 and 18 m;
the circular route is centred near (-186.7, -1) m. These values are alignment
inputs, not surveyed measurements. The working paved correction spans X -245 to
-136 m and Y -25 to 25 m, retaining the existing bounded elevation controls.
The pavilion footprints are seed outlines with 1–3 m estimated uncertainty.
Precise levels, stair/ramp access, furniture placements, boundary openings,
planting and individual pavilion elevations need closer evidence and checking
against neighbouring exported meshes. No inscription has been read.

## Status

The ground correction replaces the broad provisional lawn inside the
bounded civic area with paving. A simplified checker strip uses estimated 2 by
1 m cells on the garden axis; it is cut out of the underlying paving and
reserves a 10 m radius around the fountain for later reconstruction. It does
**not** yet reconstruct the fountain, pavilions, stonework, planting, or street
furniture. It must not be
presented as completing the snag. Ground, accessibility, the two placeholder
buildings and all three assembled viewer levels still require detailed work and
visual review.

## Follow-up evidence and provisional metric schedule (26 October 2026)

An actual Google Maps satellite image was inspected at 19z, centred at
53.64743, -3.0064. The displayed 20 m scale bar is approximately 93 image
pixels. The broad outer circular motif spans approximately 108 pixels,
corresponding to 23 m (uncertainty at least 2 m from edge identification and
screen measurement). The central blue pool spans approximately 29 pixels,
about 6.2 m (uncertainty at least 1 m). The patterned axial strip appears about
25–28 pixels wide, roughly 5–6 m; its cells are considerably smaller than the
present 2 m provisional cells. These measurements describe the image rather
than a ground survey. The screenshot, including the scale bar, is held privately
at `../output/diana-massing/reference-satellite.png`.

Stephen Craven's 22 September 2025 photograph was actually inspected. It shows
at least two low risers around the circular fountain platform and a radial
break/approach; the much larger surrounding ornament is paving, not an outer
raised wall. The temporary timber planters are a later layer. The pool surface
is horizontal and its platform, landing and approaches need a designed connection
to the sloping road datum, which varies by several tenths of a metre across this
area. No precise riser heights or compliant ramp gradient have yet been measured.

Actual Street View July 2026, from Eastbank Street, shows the southern pavilion's
long shallow hipped/faceted roof, dark slender structural framing, arched glazed
bays and pale low plinth. Actual July 2019 imagery from Lord Street shows rows of
benches and pale entrance columns and balustrade. The north pavilion and its
far elevations need closer views. The [Southport Civic Society heritage
trail](https://www.southporttownscape.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Southport-Lord-Street-trail.pdf)
describes a cast-iron and glass former Tourist Information pavilion and a
later complementary pavilion in front of Town Gardens Café. The trail places
the former across Eastbank Street, so it must not be assumed to identify the
south pavilion within this snag. The adjacent older
[classical pavilion](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101379671-pavilion-in-municipal-gardens-dukes-ward)
is a separate structure and is not assumed to be either polygonal shelter.

The browser session opened Google 3D but displayed “3D Earth view is not
available”; actual satellite and street panoramas did display. Alternate

## Current construction and remaining review

The source ground now owns a framed checker strip, four cells across at an
estimated 0.9 m cell width, with two narrow dark bands on each side. Eight
alternating radial bands occupy the 7.2–11.5 m radii. These are flush surfaces
cut into the pavement; the raised fountain has three estimated 0.14 m risers,
a level platform and a horizontal 3.2 m radius water surface. The inner platform
is cut out of the ordinary pavement and its outer collar joins the road datum.
The estimated 2 m wide, approximately 1:16–1:19 rising connection is on the
Atkinson side and breaks the risers there. Its exact location and geometry
are an interpretation, not an observation or a statement of regulatory
compliance, and need independent review. The 2025 photograph suggests a break in the steps, but does not establish
this ramp's surveyed station, existence or grade.

The two bounded Lord Street planting beds, four proxy tree canopies, bench rows,
open balustrade runs, globe piers and paired entrance columns are estimated
from the 2017 and 2019 views; exact inventories, species, stations and planting
are not measured. There is no claim that the regular temporary 2025 planters
were present in 2017. Furniture streams separately from the walking ground;
blender checks cover every face of each pavilion, representative garden
prototypes, adjacent balustrade modules and the bounded ground surfaces.

The new separately owned pavilion asset uses the original polygonal seeds for
registration. Its entrances, eave/roof heights and bay schedule are provisional.
Close views support their arched fanlights, pale lower panels, structural posts,
projecting canopies and cresting, but not all rear faces or door positions.
No tenant sign is included. The former placeholder volumes are marked for
replacement in the same future publication. The integrated ground, pavilions and placeholder removal are published locally;
independent final acceptance and snag resolution remain pending.

The checked ground package currently measures 85,506 / 54,258 / 25,314

## Local publication and review (26 October 2026)

The ground, two replacements and placeholder removal were published together.
Actual pavilion totals, north plus south, are 29,921 / 25,993 / 290 triangles
and 3.76 / 3.30 / 0.136 MB for Detail / Walk / District. The District GPU
estimate is 2.13 MB for the two bounded 256 px atlases and geometry. The
package checker verifies actual exported budgets, silhouettes and coplanar
exterior contacts. The assembled exported audit checks garden sections 05–07,
pavilions, Town Hall, Christ Church approach and Eastbank ground at all three
levels; it found no same-facing cross-part overlaps. These checks do not prove
that every approach is surveyed or visually correct.

The live viewer showed the actual Detail, Walk and District pavilion and
furniture tiles. Ground is identical across levels; its collapsed tile was
labelled Walk when District was forced. A short axial Walk movement followed
the ground. Opposite southern and rear northern pavilion views and nearby
ground joins were inspected. A first District view exposed inward receiver
normals on the north pavilion, producing a dark blank facade despite complete
projection coverage. Corrected normals and a direction regression restored
its window appearance in the republished District view. Top-down tread edges
show intermittent fine dark pixels, and the north garden boundary has an
abrupt paving material change; these remain visible review limitations.
Private captures are in `../output/diana-assembled-review/viewer-*.png`.
The snag remains in progress pending independent final acceptance.
