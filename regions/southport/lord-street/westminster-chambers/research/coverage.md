# Westminster Chambers, 106–108 Lord Street: evidence and coverage

Research 2026-10-26. June the Florist's own contact page gives 108 Lord Street. Google Maps' address search and the loaded August 2024 panorama show the June sign under Westminster Chambers' corner veranda; a July 2026 Eastbank panorama shows the same name around the return. The business occupies the ground floor of the red brick corner block, not the adjoining modern Vincent Hotel. A 106–108 commercial listing calls this Westminster Chambers, five storeys, and places it next to the Vincent. The 631–637 Lord Street Historic England shop photograph is a different historical premises and is not used for this building.

- Lord Street: Google Street View, Aug 2024, panorama CcFvDuRvpXlpXbgeQ87Ubw, location 53.6468688,-3.0071113. Two upper storeys over the shop; cream polygonal oriels, brick piers, balustrades and a deep projecting upper balcony with brackets; curved gables and dormers in slate roof. Fascia reads JUNE THE FLORIST. The glazed entrance lies slightly left of the fascia centre. The veranda has white pierced cast-iron spandrels, dark bases and white columns, and extends beyond the unit. Window display contents are transient and not surveyed.
- Eastbank Street: Google Street View, July 2026, panorama Ta2TDJwA_HrkppCPqfPHng at 53.646953,-3.0068572. A long return with red brick, multiple oriels, upper sash windows, bands, iron balconies, gables and chimneys. Veranda turns the corner. Other shop signs changed with tenants; only readable evidenced names should be used.
- Roof and rear: Google Maps 3D photogrammetry, accessed 2026-10-26, two opposing obliques and near-overhead. The block is a separate roof from the flat-roofed Vincent. Main slate roofs form an irregular junction with hipped sections around a recessed service yard; tall chimneys and dormers. Hodge Street flank is red brick with several openings. Photogrammetry is distorted; details, exact valley edges and rear openings are uncertain. Captures held privately in the snag directory.
- Footprint and terrain: site frame aligned to Lord Street, origin 53.6486555,-3.0043401, yaw 43.5293 degrees. Address pin projects approximately to x=-273, y=-27 m. The prior OSM-derived way-371140808-vincent partition spans x approximately -292 to -266 and y -47 to -23, but its label is misleading; use it as a registration hypothesis only. Aerial and 3D confirm a roughly 20 m by 21 m main corner and a recessed rear toward the Vincent; roof overhangs and the private rear courtyard make exact wall boundaries uncertain by 1–3 m. Datum near x=-275,y=-27 is +0.101 m from the lord-corridor pavement model, itself estimated. Four full visible levels including ground plus attic; eaves and ridge are estimated from street ratios pending measurement, not surveyed.
- Neighbours: the larger modern Vincent immediately southwest is separate and must retain its placeholder. Hodge Street is an access lane on the rear flank; do not close it. Public pavement is owned by corridor-public-realm. The veranda across the boundary needs one owner per span.
- Missing: no ground-level rear/service-yard photograph inspected; exact plan and party wall, internals, skylight arrangement, chimney shapes and small inscription details remain uncertain. No photo texture is distributed.

Historic England list entry 1379607 (https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1379607, listed 1999; accessed 2026-10-26) identifies the full architectural unit as 102–108 and 106A Lord Street and 2, 2A and 4 Eastbank Street. It records a rectangular corner plan, three main storeys plus two attic levels, a raised slated mansard, five-sided two-storey oriels, Dutch gables, octagonal corner turret, modillion cornice and iron balconies. The Eastbank return has five bays and different bay heights; its appearance cannot simply be mirrored from Lord Street. The model uses a raised two-tier mansard, a corner turret and the observed main facade bay divisions. Rear lightwell shape remains an inference from overhead imagery rather than a surveyed boundary.

Measurement inputs: the Google plan capture `june-3d-plan.png` has a 10 m scale at lower right. The Lord Street frontage from turret to Vincent boundary is roughly twice that bar (~20 m); Eastbank frontage is similarly around 20 m. The old polygon gives the independent bounds -286.4 to -266.24 (20.16 m), -46.42 to -25.45 (20.97 m); parallax and canopy projection mean these are not a survey. The street images place the main cornice above ground plus two upper floors; the first model's 11.5 m cornice and 15–16 m roof height are provisional estimates, not measured image-calibrated values. The widened gable/roof footprint, 5-sided oriels, attic windows, turret and roof junction remain to be aligned to matched imagery. A web search for Hodge Street Mapillary and image sources did not yield an inspected rear photograph; the absence of a match does not mean those elevations are blank.

The listed-building photograph gives a lower attic break roughly 90–100 image pixels above the main cornice, and the central Dutch gable crown roughly 75–85 pixels above that break and just below the upper dormer heads. With the estimated 2 m lower attic tier, the crown is modelled at about 15.1 m above datum; these are image ratios, not a measured survey. The upper canted bays are offset from the gables as seen in the front photo.
