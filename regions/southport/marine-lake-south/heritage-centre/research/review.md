# Current exterior review

The target is the Heritage Centre Café in Princes Park, at the seaward end of
the Venetian Bridge. It is not the separate Marine Lake Café near the pier.
Google Maps' location label and dated place photos identify the selected parcel;
OSM way 170138154 supplies the initial outline. The retained source-geometry
record preserves that seed while the public-realm ground cutout follows the
modelled walls and rear drum. The approach apron is owned and clipped by the
public-realm asset, and the bridge deck and lower path remain separate owners.

A 22.34 m main span and 12–13 m depth were derived by projecting the OSM
vertices onto the building's rotated local axes. The rear drum is approximately
8.1 m across. The reference photos constrain the ratio of the ~2 m doorway to
the approximately 2.7 m outer eaves, 3.8 m inner wing and 4.65 m central barrel;
these are estimated to ±0.5 m, with local ground on the project's rough datum.
The front green utility door, panel joints, louvres, deeper green fascia, curved
canopy, entry glazing, fluted kiosk and legible permanent sign follow the
2017–2025 views. The 2017 sign/door state and the 2025 service signs varied;
temporary price posters and interiors are not represented. The translucent
canopy is rendered with opaque pale materials, a known approximation.

The native front, entry and oblique renders were compared with Jomo's June 2017
front/entry photographs and Tilly's October 2025 cafe photographs; the long side
was compared with Scott Spencer's July 2020 sphere. Opposing 3D orbits were used
for roof and rear silhouette. The rear drum and rear wings lack close ground-level
coverage; their smooth surfaces are restrained approximations, not evidence of
absent openings. Green roof colour, exact cladding dimensions and positions,
roof weathering, grade and canopy transparency remain estimates.

The declared mesh validator checks the shell, roof, glazing, joinery, kiosk,
lettering and small fittings for same-facing coplanar overlaps and degenerate
triangles. The independent 512-pixel District receiver has a fixed 1,200-triangle
budget. In the assembled viewer, the entrance, apron, rear drum and bridge
approach were reviewed with the cafe's Detail, Walk and District tiles resident.
The apron regression probes 20 approach points and 22 wall/drum perimeter points
for a sole ground owner; existing bridge contact and opening checks run on all
three exported public-realm levels. These checks do not certify a surveyed site
or establish hidden rear construction.
